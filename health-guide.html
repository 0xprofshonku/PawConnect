Rabies: prevention is essential

Rabies is a serious viral disease that affects mammals, including dogs and humans. Exposure may occur through bites or saliva entering broken skin or the eyes or mouth.

Never attempt to examine a suspected rabid animal yourself. If a person is bitten or scratched, wash the area with soap and running water for 15 minutes and seek urgent medical assessment for rabies prevention.

Skin problems and mange

Hair loss, scabs and itching may indicate parasites, infection or other skin conditions. A veterinarian can determine the cause and recommend suitable treatment.

Avoid applying household chemicals or human medicines to a dog's skin.

Helping dogs during winter

Provide a dry, sheltered place protected from wind and rain. Keep bedding dry and offer clean drinking water and appropriate food.

Persistent shivering, weakness, confusion or unresponsiveness can indicate a serious problem and require urgent veterinary help.

Injuries and accidents

Serious bleeding, difficulty breathing, inability to stand or major trauma require urgent veterinary assistance.

Keep yourself safe, avoid unnecessary handling and contact a veterinarian or animal rescue service for instructions.

Infectious diseases

Canine distemper and parvovirus can cause severe illness. Signs such as repeated vomiting, bloody diarrhea, breathing problems or marked weakness need prompt veterinary assessment.

Vaccination is an important prevention measure. Do not diagnose a disease from symptoms alone.

Prevention and everyday care

Communities can support street dogs through suitable food, clean water, vaccination, humane population management and veterinary care when needed.

Seen a dog who needs help?

Share the dog's location and condition so appropriate local help can be contacted.
